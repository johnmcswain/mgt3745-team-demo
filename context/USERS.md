# USERS.md

Three profiles merged from four members' HW2 research. Each names the research it rests on. The profiles are composites; names are invented.

## Research Sources

| Member | HW2 research | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| Priya | Two interviews with landscaping business owners (Mom Test format) | Rosa |
| Leo | One interview with a bookkeeper at a family-owned HVAC company, including a walk-through of her Monday receivables routine | Tom |
| Dana | One ride-along interview with a field supervisor for a lawn-care company | Jamal |
| Marcus | Review of twelve app-store reviews of field-service apps, filtered to complaints about data entry | Jamal (constraints) |

## Profile 1: Rosa Delgado, Owner-Operator

- **Context.** Owns three locations. Answers the phone from her truck. Checks receivables on Sunday nights.
- **Job statement.** When a customer tells a crew lead they will pay by Friday, I want that promise to reach me the same day, so I can follow up before it becomes a 45-day problem.
- **What she said.** She usually learns on day 30 that a customer told a crew lead weeks earlier that the check was coming. (Priya's interview 1, paraphrased.)
- **Success looks like.** She stops being surprised by promises.

## Profile 2: Tom Nguyen, Office Manager

- **Context.** Runs receivables for all three locations every Monday morning from a spreadsheet exported from the accounting system.
- **Job statement.** When I run Monday receivables, I want to see which overdue invoices have a promise attached and which do not, so I can call the silent ones first.
- **What he does now.** Calls every overdue customer in invoice order, including the ones who already told a crew lead when they would pay.
- **Success looks like.** His call list is sorted by what he does not know.

## Profile 3: Jamal Ortiz, Crew Lead

- **Context.** Leads a four-person crew, six or seven properties a day. Phone in a chest pocket, gloves on, often poor signal at rural properties.
- **Job statement.** When a customer brings up the bill on site, I want to record what they said in under 20 seconds with gloves on, so I can get back to work without becoming the collections department.
- **Constraint from Marcus's review.** The most frequent complaint across twelve field-service app reviews was the number of taps needed to log anything. Four of the twelve named login screens specifically.
- **Success looks like.** Logging a promise is faster than remembering to tell the office.

## Affected, Never a User

**Customers.** They are affected by the system and never interact with it. Nothing in Phase 2 sends a customer a message.
